# Further hypotheses, not deployed and not strength evidence

One additional hypothesis is inference from one's own across-the-table exchange. If the same opponent sent our received three and received our sent three, a sampled remaining hand plus that opponent's public plays reconstructs their post-pass thirteen. Removing our known sent cards and adding our received cards reconstructs a hypothetical original thirteen. All of this uses one's own private passing history and publicly played cards, never the actual opponent's hand.

A deliberately soft preference could favour hypothetical originals compatible with either original danger-ranking passing or the current whole-hand passing. Human passing stays unrestricted. Compatibility with either policy is allowed; incompatibility still retains positive mass. This is an uncalibrated opponent-model hypothesis, not a legality rule, and requires independent performance testing before deployment. Do not infer passing direction from secret state.


The second hypothesis is an exact information-set search in the last two tricks. It enumerates all legal remaining deals, replays all public actions to validate past legality, and searches an AND/OR tree. The root player must choose one shared action for each public history; opponents may choose any legal adversarial continuation. The certificate includes a continuation policy keyed only by the root player's observation. A hypothetical winning move that depends on knowing a hidden assignment is insufficient.

Structural checks: 24 endgames agree between alpha-beta and unpruned search and obey the perfect-information upper bound. Forty-eight certificate cases agree with the exhaustive information-set value; all 5,506 terminal continuations of 25 certificates were checked independently against legal opposing moves. Across-exchange checks retained every actual distribution in 320 observations generated by the two passing policies. These are correctness checks only. Neither hypothesis has been entered into an independent strength benchmark or deployed.
